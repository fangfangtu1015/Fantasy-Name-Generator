How to Name a D&D Character

Naming your Dungeons & Dragons character is one of the most important parts of character creation. A good name can enhance roleplay, make your character memorable, and help you connect with them on a deeper level. This guide will walk you through the process of choosing the perfect name for your D&D character.

Step 1: Consider Your Character's Race

The first step in naming your D&D character is to consider their race. Each race in D&D has its own naming conventions that reflect their culture, history, and values. Using race-appropriate names helps maintain immersion and consistency in your campaign.

Elves

Elves favor flowing, melodic names that reflect their grace and longevity. Consider using:

  • Soft consonants: L, R, S, TH, N, M
  • Flowing vowels: A, E, I combinations
  • Nature or celestial references
  • 2-3 syllables with alternating stress

Examples: Aelindor, Thalorien, Caelynn, Elora, Miravel

Dwarves

Dwarves use sturdy, traditional names inspired by Norse and Germanic languages. Consider using:

  • Hard consonants: D, G, K, R, T, B
  • Guttural clusters: GR-, KR-, DR-, BR-
  • Short, punchy syllables (1-2 syllables)
  • References to metal, stone, or craftsmanship

Examples: Durgan, Hildrun, Rogar, Brunhilda, Gunda

Orcs and Half-Orcs

Orcs use brutal, aggressive names that reflect their warlike culture. Consider using:

  • Harsh consonants: K, G, R, B, D, T
  • Short syllables (1-2 syllables)
  • Guttural sounds and aggressive rhythm
  • Clan names or warrior epithets

Examples: Grukk, Krag, Thok, Mog, Shara

Dragons and Dragonborn

Dragons use majestic, powerful names with sibilant sounds. Consider using:

  • Sibilant consonants: S, SH, Z, X
  • Powerful consonants: R, K, G, TH
  • Long, flowing syllables (2-4 syllables)
  • Titles that describe power or age

Examples: Valthorax, Ignaxarion, Khorvax, Serzinthia, Pyrathos

Tieflings

Tieflings have two naming traditions: infernal names or virtue names. Consider using:

  • Infernal: Exotic consonants (X, Z, V, TH), unusual combinations
  • Virtue: Abstract concepts (Hope, Faith, Glory, Redemption)

Examples: Ariostos, Baalzephon, Celestia, Hope, Faith, Glory

Drow (Dark Elves)

Drow use alien, dangerous-sounding names with unusual letter combinations. Consider using:

  • Unusual consonants: X, Z, V, Q
  • Apostrophes to create glottal stops
  • Harsh clusters: SZ-, ZH-, XZ-, VR-
  • Female names are longer, male names are shorter

Examples: Zedra, Vyracon, Szoleth, Liriel, Zesstra

Step 2: Consider Your Character's Class

While race is the most important factor, your character's class can also influence their name. Consider:

  • Warriors and fighters: Names that sound strong and battle-ready
  • Mages and wizards: Names with mystical or arcane-sounding elements
  • Rogues and assassins: Names with shadow or stealth connotations
  • Clerics and paladins: Names with religious or noble undertones
  • Rangers and druids: Names that reference nature or the wild
  • Bards: Names that sound musical or poetic

Step 3: Consider Your Character's Background

Your character's background can provide additional naming inspiration:

  • Noble: Use formal, elaborate names with titles
  • Sage: Use names that sound wise or scholarly
  • Soldier: Use names that sound martial or battle-hardened
  • Outlander: Use names from their homeland's culture
  • Criminal: Use names with shadowy or dangerous connotations
  • Folk Hero: Use names that sound approachable and heroic

Step 4: Consider Your Character's Personality

The sound of a name can reflect your character's personality:

  • Serious and stoic: Use names with hard consonants and short syllables
  • Graceful and elegant: Use names with flowing vowels and soft consonants
  • Mysterious and enigmatic: Use names with unusual letter combinations
  • Cheerful and outgoing: Use names with bright, open vowel sounds
  • Dark and brooding: Use names with shadow or night references

Step 5: Choose a Surname

Not all D&D characters use surnames, but if yours does, consider:

  • Elves: Compound words referencing nature, stars, or family heritage
  • Dwarves: Clan names referencing metal, stone, or ancestral deeds
  • Humans: Occupational names, location names, or patronymics
  • Half-orcs: May use human or orc surnames, or none at all
  • Tieflings: May use infernal surnames or virtue names
  • Drow: Noble house names with distinctive Underdark conventions

Step 6: Test the Name

Before finalizing your character's name, test it:

  1. Say it out loud: Can you pronounce it easily? Will your DM and fellow players be able to say it?
  2. Test the full name: Do the first and last names sound good together?
  3. Check for conflicts: Are there any other characters in the party with similar-sounding names?
  4. Consider the setting: Does the name fit the campaign world and tone?
  5. Get feedback: Ask your DM or fellow players what they think of the name

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using names that are too long or complex: If you can't pronounce it easily, neither can anyone else
  • Using names that don't fit the race: An elf named "Grom" or An elf named "Lilybelle Starwhisper" breaks immersion
  • Using names that are too similar to other characters: Avoid having two characters named "Aralion" and "Aralien" in the same party
  • Using names from popular culture: Naming your character after famous fictional characters can be distracting
  • Ignoring the campaign setting: A name that works in one setting might not work in another

Conclusion

Naming your D&D character is an important part of bringing them to life. By considering their race, class, background, and personality, you can choose a name that enhances roleplay, maintains immersion, and makes your character memorable.
