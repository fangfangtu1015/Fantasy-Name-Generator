How to Pronounce Fantasy Names

Fantasy names can be intimidating. With their unusual letter combinations, apostrophes in strange places, and sounds that don't exist in English, it's easy to feel unsure about how to say them correctly. This guide will teach you the rules and patterns behind fantasy name pronunciation so you can confidently say any fantasy name.

Basic Pronunciation Rules

Most fantasy names follow these basic rules:

  1. Vowels are usually pronounced: Unlike English, where vowels can be silent, fantasy names typically pronounce every vowel
  2. Stress usually falls on the first or second syllable: Most fantasy names are stressed on the first syllable (THOR-al) or second syllable (el-RON-diel)
  3. Consonant clusters are pronounced separately: Each consonant in a cluster gets its own sound (DRIZZ-ek, not "jritsek")
  4. Apostrophes indicate a pause or glottal stop: They break up syllables (Vael'ara = VAY-el-AR-a with a pause after "Vay-el")

Elvish Pronunciation

Elvish names are typically melodic and flowing, with stress on the first or second syllable:

  • Aelindor: ay-EL-in-dor (stress on second syllable)
  • Thalorien: THAL-or-ee-en (stress on first syllable)
  • Elora: el-OR-ah (stress on second syllable)
  • Caelynn: KAY-lin (stress on first syllable)
  • Miravel: meer-ah-VEL (stress on third syllable)
  • Thalindra: thal-IN-dra (stress on second syllable)

Key patterns: Flowing vowels, soft consonants, melodic rhythm

Dwarvish Pronunciation

Dwarvish names are typically short and punchy, with hard stress on the first syllable:

  • Durgan: DUR-gan (stress on first syllable, hard "g")
  • Hildrun: HIL-drun (stress on first syllable)
  • Rogar: RO-gar (stress on first syllable)
  • Brunhilda: brun-HIL-da (stress on second syllable)
  • Gunda: GUN-da (stress on first syllable)
  • Fenrik: FEN-rik (stress on first syllable, rhymes with "hen")

Key patterns: Hard consonants, short syllables, guttural sounds

Orcish Pronunciation

Orcish names are typically harsh and guttural, with aggressive stress:

  • Grukk: GRUK (one syllable, hard "g" and "k")
  • Krag: KRAG (one syllable, hard "k" and "g")
  • Thok: THOK (one syllable, "th" as in "thorn")
  • Mog: MOG (one syllable, hard "g")
  • Shara: SHAR-ah (stress on first syllable)
  • Krell: KREL (one syllable, hard "k" and "ll")

Key patterns: Hard consonants, short syllables, aggressive sounds

Draconic Pronunciation

Draconic names are typically long and majestic, with sibilant sounds:

  • Valthorax: val-THOR-aks (stress on second syllable)
  • Ignaxarion: ig-nak-SAR-ee-on (stress on third syllable)
  • Pyrathos: peer-AH-thos (stress on second syllable)
  • Serzinthia: ser-ZIN-thee-ah (stress on second syllable)
  • Khorvax: KOR-vaks (one syllable, "au" as in "cow")
  • Iymrath: EEM-rath (stress on first syllable)

Key patterns: Sibilant sounds, long syllables, majestic rhythm

Drow Pronunciation

Drow names are typically alien and exotic, with unusual letter combinations:

  • Zedra: ZED-ra (stress on first syllable, "z" as in "zoo")
  • Vyracon: vir-ah-KON (stress on third syllable)
  • Szoleth: SO-leth (stress on first syllable, "sz" as in "sit")
  • Liriel: LEE-ree-el (stress on first syllable)
  • Zesstra: ZESS-tra (stress on first syllable, "ss" as in "hiss")
  • Nyzra: NYZ-ra (stress on first syllable, "y" as in "my")

Key patterns: Unusual consonants (X, Z, V, Q), apostrophes, exotic sounds

Tiefling Pronunciation

Tiefling names vary between infernal and virtue traditions:

Infernal Names

  • Ariostos: ar-ee-OS-tos (stress on third syllable)
  • Baalzephon: BAL-ze-fon (stress on first syllable)
  • Celestia: sel-ES-ti-a (stress on second syllable)
  • Damara: DAM-ah-ra (stress on first syllable)
  • Erebus: EHR-eh-bus (stress on first syllable)

Virtue Names

  • Hope: HOPE (one syllable, pronounced like the English word)
  • Faith: FAYTH (one syllable, pronounced like the English word)
  • Glory: GLO-ree (stress on first syllable)
  • Redemption: re-DEMP-shun (stress on second syllable)

Handling Apostrophes

Apostrophes in fantasy names typically indicate one of three things:

  1. Glottal stop: A brief pause in the middle of a word (Vael'ara = VAY-el-AR-a with a pause after "Vay-el")
  2. Syllable break: Separating two syllables that would otherwise blend together (D'Vral = duh-VRAL)
  3. Elision: Indicating that a letter or sound has been dropped (O'Brien = oh-BRY-en, not oh-bree-en)

Examples:

  • Vael'ara: VAY-el-AR-a (pause after "Vay-el")
  • D'Vral: duh-VRAL (syllable break)
  • Shi'nayne: shee-NAYN (elision)
  • T'risstree: TRIS-tree (elision)

Handling Unusual Letter Combinations

Fantasy names often use letter combinations that don't appear in English. Here's how to pronounce them:

  • Ae: Usually pronounced "ay" as in "say" (Aelindor = ay-EL-in-dor)
  • Au: Usually pronounced "ow" as in "cow" (Valthorax = val-THOR-aks)
  • Ea: Usually pronounced "eh-ah" or "ee-ah" (Ealarion = eh-alar-ee-ON)
  • Ie: Usually pronounced "ee-eh" or "eye-eh" (Liriel = LEE-ree-el)
  • Oe: Usually pronounced "oh-eh" or "oo-eh" (Fenrik = FEN-rik)
  • Ue: Usually pronounced "oo-eh" (Thuon = THOO-on)
  • Y: Can be pronounced "ee" as in "see" or "ih" as in "sit" depending on position (Liriel = LEE-ree-el, Grynn = GRIN)
  • Sz: Usually pronounced "s" as in "sit" (Szoleth = SO-leth)
  • Zs: Usually pronounced "zh" as in "measure" (Zsolt = ZHOLT)
  • Th: Can be voiced as in "this" or unvoiced as in "thorn" (Thalorien = THAL-or-ee-en)
  • Ph: Usually pronounced "f" as in "fish" (Elphaba = el-FAH-bah)
  • Gh: Can be silent, "g" as in "go", or "f" as in "fish" depending on origin (Ghim = GIM, Ghâsh = GAASH)

Stress and Accent Placement

Knowing where to place stress is crucial for correct pronunciation:

  • First syllable stress: Most common in dwarvish and orcish names (DUR-gan, GRUK, KRAG)
  • Second syllable stress: Common in elvish and tiefling names (thal-OR-en, vi-KO-ni-a)
  • Third syllable stress: Less common, but appears in longer names (gal-ah-DREE-el, ar-ee-OS-tos)
  • Final syllable stress: Rare in fantasy names, but appears in some elvish names (mir-ah-VEL)

Practice Tips

To improve your fantasy name pronunciation:

  1. Break names into syllables: Separate each syllable and practice them individually before combining
  2. Identify the stress: Figure out which syllable gets the emphasis
  3. Practice aloud: Say the name out loud multiple times until it feels natural
  4. Use our audio feature: Click the "Listen" button on our name generators to hear names pronounced
  5. Ask for help: If you're unsure, ask your DM or fellow players how they pronounce a name

Common Pronunciation Mistakes

Avoid these common mistakes:

  • Ignoring vowels: Don't skip vowels just because they look unusual
  • Misplacing stress: Make sure you're emphasizing the right syllable
  • Anglicizing sounds: Don't try to make fantasy names sound like English words
  • Ignoring apostrophes: Apostrophes indicate pauses or breaks—don't skip them
  • Rushing through names: Take your time to pronounce each syllable clearly

Conclusion

Pronouncing fantasy names doesn't have to be intimidating. By understanding the basic rules of vowel pronunciation, stress placement, and unusual letter combinations, you can confidently say any fantasy name. Practice regularly, use our audio features, and don't be afraid to ask for help. With time, you'll be pronouncing even the most exotic drow names like a native of the Underdark.
