High Elf Names for Your D&D Campaign

High Elves in D&D 5e

High elves are an elf subrace in D&D 5e. The traits below are from the 2014 Player's Handbook (p.23):

  • Ability Score Increase: Dexterity +2, Intelligence +1
  • Speed: 30 feet
  • Cantrip: You know one wizard cantrip of your choice. Intelligence is your spellcasting ability for it
  • Weapon proficiencies: Longsword, shortsword, shortbow, longbow (same as all elves)
  • Darkvision, Fey Ancestry, Trance: Same as all elves (60 ft darkvision, advantage vs. charm, immune to magical sleep, 4-hour trance)

Compared to wood elves, high elves lean toward arcane study: they get Intelligence +1 and a wizard cantrip where wood elves get Wisdom +1 and Mask of the Wild. In the Forgotten Realms, high elves are often scholars, mages, or nobles in ancient cities.

On the 2024 rules: The 2024 Player's Handbook revises character creation — ability scores are no longer tied to species, and several subrace traits change. The 2014 high elf traits above may not apply as written under the 2024 rules. Check which ruleset your campaign uses before building a character.

Naming: Neither the 2014 nor the 2024 Player's Handbook publishes a separate name table for high elves. High elf characters use the general elf name tables (2014 PHB p.23).

High Elves in Other Fantasy Settings

High elves or their equivalents appear in several major fantasy settings beyond D&D. Naming conventions differ:

Tolkien's Noldor (High Elves)

  • The Noldor are Tolkien's "Deep Elves" or "High Elves" — exiles from Valinor who returned to Middle-earth. They are associated with craft, scholarship, and lore.
  • Known Noldor: Galadriel, Finrod Felagund, Glorfindel, Fëanor, Celebrimbor.
  • Quenya is the language of the Noldor in Valinor; Sindarin became the common Elvish speech in Middle-earth. Most Noldor in Middle-earth used Sindarin names.
  • Tolkien constructed Quenya and Sindarin as full languages with their own phonology. To create original names in this style, study the sound patterns (Quenya favors long vowels, L/R/T/D consonants, and specific consonant clusters) and build new names from those elements. Changing one letter of a known character name ("Galadriel" → "Galadrial") is not original naming.

Warhammer High Elves (Asur)

  • The Asur live on the island continent of Ulthuan.
  • Teclis is one of the greatest mages in the setting; Tyrion is his twin, a peerless warrior; Alarielle is the Everqueen.
  • Asur names tend to be long and elaborate, often referencing gods (Asuryan, Lileath) or places.
  • Warhammer high elf names carry a formal, aristocratic cadence.

Elder Scrolls Altmer (High Elves)

  • The Altmer live on the Summerset Isles and consider themselves the closest mortal descendants of the gods.
  • The Thalmor is an Altmer political and military faction. Known NPCs: Ancano, Elenwen, Ondolemar.
  • Altmer names tend to be long and sometimes use apostrophes. The Elder Scrolls games do not publish an official Altmer naming table.
  • To build an original Altmer-style name, keep it multi-syllabic and favor formal, flowing sounds. Treat game NPC names as sound references, not as names to copy directly.

Building a High Elf Name (Practical Approach)

Since D&D 5e doesn't have a separate high elf name table, here are practical approaches:

Option A — Use the PHB elf table directly. Pick a name from the PHB elf list above. Add a family name if your character has one. This is the simplest and most "official" approach. A high elf wizard named "Quarion Amastacia" uses only official PHB names.

Option B — Build an arcane or celestial compound name (homebrew). Many players and DMs give high elves compound names referencing magic, stars, or light (Starweaver, Dawnforger, Moonwhisper). This is a popular community convention, not a rule in the PHB. If you use it:

  • Combine a celestial or arcane word (Star, Moon, Dawn, Light, Spell, Rune, Glyph) with a role word (weaver, forger, keeper, warden, binder, caller).
  • Keep it pronounceable. "Starweaver" works; "Astrologicaweaver" does not.
  • Be consistent within a house or family.

Option C — Borrow a sound profile from a published setting. If you like the feel of Tolkien's Noldor names but can't use them directly, extract the sound profile (Quenya/Sindarin favors L, R, and flowing vowels; few harsh consonants) and build new names from those elements. Don't just respell "Galadriel" as "Galadrial."

Naming a High Elf House or Community

When naming a group (a noble house, a council, a magical academy), consistency matters more than any single name:

PHB-style house: Use one family name from the PHB list for all members. Example:

  • Quarion Amastacia
  • Ielenia Amastacia
  • Laucian Amastacia

(Amastacia translates to "Starflower")

Arcane-compound house: Pick a theme and vary the role word. Example house "Star":

  • Quarion Starweaver
  • Ielenia Starkeeper
  • Laucian Starcaller
  • Erevan Starwarden

Mixed council: A high elf council may include characters with PHB family names, arcane-compound names, or titles (Archmage, Sage, Lorekeeper). Titles precede the name in formal address. The inconsistency between members is fine — it reflects different houses and traditions within the same society.

How to Choose and Refine Your Name

  1. Decide your naming convention. PHB elf names, arcane compounds, or a mix. Pick one and stay consistent within a house.
  2. Say it aloud. If you can't pronounce it quickly at the table, simplify. Long high elf names often get shortened in play — make sure the short form works.
  3. Check for duplicates in your party. Elf names with similar sounds (Sariel / Sariia) cause confusion.
  4. Match the name to the character's story. A high elf scholar may carry a long family name. One who travels incognito may use only a first name or a childhood nickname.
  5. Don't respell famous names. If you like "Galadriel," extract its sound profile and build something new. "Galadrial" is not original naming.
  6. Consider your setting. D&D 5e uses PHB elf names. Tolkien's Noldor use Sindarin/Quenya. Warhammer Asur use long, formal names. Pick the convention that fits your game.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does D&D 5e have a separate high elf name table?

No. High elves are an elf subrace. Both the 2014 Player's Handbook (p.23) and the 2024 revision provide a general elf name table used for all elf subraces, not a separate high elf table.

What's the difference between high elf and wood elf in D&D 5e?

In the 2014 PHB, the difference is in rules traits, not naming: high elves get Intelligence +1 and a wizard cantrip; wood elves get Wisdom +1, 35 ft speed, and Mask of the Wild. Both subraces use the same PHB elf name table. The common table convention of giving high elves "arcane" names and wood elves "nature" names is a community choice, not a PHB rule.

Are "Auralion" and "Starweaver" official D&D high elf names?

No. These celestial/arcane compound names are a popular homebrew convention. The closest official equivalent is the PHB elf family names that translate to luminous compounds in Common (Amastacia = Starflower, Galanodel = Moonwhisper, Naïlo = Nightbreeze).

Can I use these names in my D&D campaign?

Names generated by this tool are original combinations. Named characters from published settings (Galadriel, Finrod, Teclis) belong to their respective works. PHB elf names are from the D&D 5e Player's Handbook. For published or commercial work, check the relevant licenses.
