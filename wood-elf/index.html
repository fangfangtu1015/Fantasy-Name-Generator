Wood Elf Names for Your D&D Campaign

Wood Elves in D&D 5e

Wood elves (also called forest elves or sylvan elves) are an elf subrace in D&D 5e. The traits below are from the 2014 Player's Handbook (p.23):

  • Ability Score Increase: Dexterity +2, Wisdom +1
  • Speed: 35 feet (faster than other elves)
  • Mask of the Wild: You can attempt to hide while only lightly obscured by foliage, heavy rain, falling snow, mist, or other natural phenomena
  • Cantrips: None (high elves get a wizard cantrip; wood elves do not)
  • Weapon proficiencies: Longsword, shortsword, shortbow, longbow (same as all elves)

In the Forgotten Realms, wood elves are common. They tend to live in deep forests and are often described as warm-hearted but somewhat aloof or feral-seeming to outsiders.

On the 2024 rules: The 2024 Player's Handbook revises character creation — ability scores are no longer tied to species, and several subrace traits change. The 2014 wood elf traits above may not apply as written under the 2024 rules. Check which ruleset your campaign uses before building a character.

Naming: Neither the 2014 nor the 2024 Player's Handbook publishes a separate name table for wood elves. Wood elf characters use the general elf name tables (2014 PHB p.23).

Wood Elves in Other Fantasy Settings

Wood elves or forest elves appear in several major fantasy settings beyond D&D. Naming conventions differ:

Tolkien's Silvan / Woodland Elves

  • The Silvan Elves of Mirkwood (Thranduil's realm) and the Galadhrim of Lothlórien are the best-known "wood elves" in fantasy.
  • Legolas and Thranduil are characters in Tolkien's published works. Tauriel appears only in Peter Jackson's film adaptations, not in Tolkien's writings.
  • Tolkien constructed full Elvish languages (Quenya and Sindarin) with their own phonology and word-building rules. Sindarin is the language associated with the woodland elves of Middle-earth.
  • To create original names in this style, study the sound patterns (Sindarin favors L, R, TH, and soft vowels; it uses few harsh consonants) and build new names from those elements. Changing one letter of a known character name ("Legolas" → "Legolath") is not original naming.

Warhammer Wood Elves

  • Live in Athel Loren, a magical forest.
  • Orion (king) and Ariel (queen) are seasonal figures — Orion dies at the winter solstice and is reborn in spring. This is a real example of a seasonal rebirth cycle tied to a wood elf king in a published setting.
  • Other names: Drycha, Naestra, Araloth.
  • Warhammer wood elf names tend to be short and harsh-edged.

Elder Scrolls Bosmer (Wood Elves)

  • The Bosmer live in Valenwood and follow the Green Pact: they do not harm the forest's plants. As a result, Bosmer culture is carnivorous and uses bone, leather, and imported materials rather than wood.
  • The Camoran dynasty ruled Valenwood. Mankar Camoran and Camoran Anaxemes are specific characters from the games, not a naming template.
  • Naming style: The Elder Scrolls games do not publish an official Bosmer naming table. Based on Bosmer NPC names across the games, Bosmer names tend to be short and rarely use apostrophes — distinct from the longer, more elaborate names of Altmer (High Elves) and the apostrophe-heavy names of Dunmer (Dark Elves).
  • To build an original Bosmer-style name, keep it short (one to two syllables), avoid apostrophes, and favor natural sounds. Treat game NPC names as sound references, not as names to copy directly.

Building a Wood Elf Name (Practical Approach)

Since D&D 5e doesn't have a separate wood elf name table, here are practical approaches:

Option A — Use the PHB elf table directly. Pick a name from the PHB elf list above. Add a family name if your character has one. This is the simplest and most "official" approach.

Option B — Build a nature-compound name (homebrew). Many players and DMs give wood elves nature-compound names (Fernwalker, Oakshield, Thornbow). This is a popular community convention, not a rule in the PHB. If you use it:

  • Combine a nature word (Oak, Fern, Thorn, Moss, Willow, Briar, Ash, Yew, Hazel, Holly) with a role word (walker, warden, shield, bow, weaver, hunter, runner).
  • Keep it pronounceable. "Oakshield" works; "Quercusroburshield" does not.
  • Be consistent within a family/clan — if one member uses a tree-based name, siblings probably do too.

Option C — Borrow a sound profile from a published setting. If you like the feel of Tolkien's Silvan names but can't use them directly, extract the sound profile (Sindarin favors L, R, TH, soft vowels; few harsh consonants) and build new names from those elements. Don't just respell "Legolas" as "Legolath."

Naming a Wood Elf Clan or Community

When naming a group (a family, a patrol, a village), consistency matters more than any single name:

PHB-style clan: Use one family name from the PHB list for all members. Example:

  • Theren Galanodel
  • Sariel Galanodel
  • Peren Galanodel

(Galanodel translates to "Moonwhisper")

Nature-compound clan: Pick a nature theme and vary the role word. Example clan "Oak":

  • Theren Oakwarden
  • Lia Oakweaver
  • Carric Oakshield
  • Erevan Oakrunner

Mixed party: A wood elf party may include characters with PHB family names, nature-compound names, or only first names. The inconsistency is fine — it reflects different family traditions within the same community.

How to Choose and Refine Your Name

  1. Decide your naming convention. PHB elf names, nature compounds, or a mix. Pick one and stay consistent within a family.
  2. Say it aloud. If you can't pronounce it quickly at the table, simplify.
  3. Check for duplicates in your party. Elf names with similar sounds (Sariel / Sariia) cause confusion.
  4. Match the name to the character's story. A wood elf raised in a city may use a simpler name. One deep in the forest may carry a family name tied to a specific grove.
  5. Don't respell famous names. If you like "Legolas," extract its sound profile and build something new. "Legolath" is not original naming.
  6. Consider your setting. D&D 5e uses PHB elf names. Tolkien's Silvan elves use Sindarin. Warhammer uses short, harsh names. Pick the convention that fits your game.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does D&D 5e have a separate wood elf name table?

No. Wood elves are an elf subrace. Both the 2014 Player's Handbook (p.23) and the 2024 revision provide a general elf name table used for all elf subraces, not a separate wood elf table.

What's the difference between wood elf and high elf names?

In D&D 5e, there is no official difference — both subraces use the same PHB elf name table (2014 p.23). The common distinction you'll see at tables (wood elves get nature-compound names like "Oakshield," high elves get more "arcane" names) is a community convention, not a rule in the PHB.

Are "Fernwalker" and "Oakshield" official D&D wood elf names?

No. These nature-compound names are a popular homebrew convention. The closest official equivalent is the PHB elf family names that translate to nature compounds in Common (Amakiir = Gemflower, Meliamne = Oakenheart, Naïlo = Nightbreeze).

Can I use these names in my D&D campaign?

Names generated by this tool are original combinations. Named characters from published settings (Legolas, Thranduil, Orion, Mankar Camoran) belong to their respective works. PHB elf names are from the D&D 5e Player's Handbook. For published or commercial work, check the relevant licenses.
