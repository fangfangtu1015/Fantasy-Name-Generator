About Drow Names

Drow — also called dark elves — are an elf subrace described in the Player's Handbook (p.23) and expanded in Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes (p.22-23). Unlike other elves, drow live in the Underdark and worship Lolth, the Spider Queen. Their naming traditions reflect this distinct culture.

This page focuses on drow names specifically. For general elf names (high elf, wood elf, eladrin), see the Elf Name Generator.

Drow Names vs Elf Names

Drow names differ from other elf names in several ways:

  • House names: Drow use House names (e.g., Do'Urden, Baenre) that reflect their political affiliation in drow society. Other elf subraces typically use family or clan names without the same political structure.
  • Apostrophes: Drow names frequently use apostrophes (Do'Urden, Baen're). This is a spelling convention in published D&D material — see the section below.
  • Underdark setting: Drow names are tied to the Underdark and drow culture. A drow character's name signals their origin in a way that a wood elf or high elf name does not.

Apostrophes in Drow Names

Apostrophes appear in many drow names in published D&D material — Drizzt Do'Urden, House Baenre, Vierna Do'Urden. The apostrophe is a spelling convention that gives drow names their distinctive look. There is no single official rule for what the apostrophe represents or how it should be pronounced. DMs and players can treat it as a glottal stop, a syllable boundary, or simply a visual marker — the choice is yours.

Drow House Names

Drow society is organized into Houses, and a drow's House name is an important part of their identity. Real examples from published D&D material:

  • House Baenre — the most powerful drow House in Menzoberranzan, appearing in R.A. Salvatore's novels and Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes
  • House Do'Urden — the House of Drizzt Do'Urden, from R.A. Salvatore's The Crystal Shard series

Houses rise and fall based on political maneuvering and Lolth's favor. A drow character's House name connects them to this political structure — or, if they are an outcast like Drizzt, marks them as separated from it.

Drow Culture and Lolth

Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes (p.22-23) describes drow society as matriarchal, organized around the worship of Lolth, the Spider Queen. Drow culture values power, ambition, and political cunning. These values shape drow naming — House names carry political weight, and a drow's name signals their place in the hierarchy.

This is background flavor, not a naming rule. A drow character can have any name that fits their backstory, regardless of whether it follows traditional drow naming patterns.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a drow name and an elf name?

Drow are a subrace of elf (PHB p.23), but their naming traditions are distinct. Drow names frequently use apostrophes (Do'Urden, Baen're) and include House names tied to drow political structure. Other elf subraces use different naming conventions — see the Elf Name Generator for general elf names.

Do drow names have to use apostrophes?

No. Apostrophes are common in published drow names but not required. A drow character can have a name without an apostrophe if it fits their backstory.

What is a drow House name?

A drow House name (like Do'Urden or Baenre) identifies the character's political House in drow society. Houses are described in Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes and in R.A. Salvatore's novels. A drow outcast may have abandoned their House name.

Can I use these names in my D&D campaign?

Names generated by this tool are original combinations. Named drow from published D&D material (Drizzt Do'Urden, House Baenre, etc.) are official D&D content.
