Fantasy Naming Traditions

Every fantasy race has distinct linguistic roots and cultural patterns that shape how their names sound. Here is a quick overview — click through to each generator for full details.

Elf Names — Flowing and Ancient

Elven names descend from Tolkien's Sindarin and Quenya, drawing on Welsh and Finnish phonology. Flowing vowels (A, E, I), liquid consonants (L, R, N), and soft fricatives (TH, S) create an elegant, ancient sound. Surnames are nature compounds: Moonwhisper, Starweaver, Thornwood.

Thalorien "Star-watch" — Thal + or + ien

Aelindra "Moon-whisper" — Ael + in + dra

Dwarf Names — Forged in Stone

Dwarven names come from Old Norse and the Dvergatal in the Poetic Edda. Hard stops (D, G, K) and guttural consonants (R, TH) create a sound carved from granite. Surnames are occupational compounds: Ironforge, Stonehammer, Deepminer.

Durgan "Enduring stone" — from Old Norse roots

Hildrun "Battle-runner" — from Germanic tradition

Dragon Names — Ancient and Commanding

Dragon names draw on Ancient Greek and Latin for their sonorous quality. Sibilant consonants (S, X, Z, TH) combined with open vowels (AY, OH, OO) create a breathy, resonant sound. Every dragon earns an epithet: the Eternal, the World-Burner, the Wise.

Ignaxarion "Fire-lord" — Igna + xar + ion

Vyraxoth "Storm-breath" — Vyr + ax + oth

Orc Names — The Sound of War

Orc names favor short, aggressive syllables built on guttural clusters (GR, KR, TH) and sharp vowels (U, O, A). Names are earned through combat, not inherited. Clan names and personal epithets announce deeds: Bloodfist, the Destroyer, Warsong.

Grukk "Bone-crusher" — single syllable, double K for impact

Kraga "Iron-claw" — Krag + a

Tiefling Names — Two Traditions, One Heritage

Tieflings have two separate naming traditions. Infernal names use exotic phonemes (Z, X, TH, PH) with long vowels. Virtue names use abstract English words: Hope, Creed, Sorrow, Glory. The choice between traditions is itself a character statement.

Azaroth "Dark flame" — infernal tradition

Redemption — virtue tradition

Drow Names — The Language of the Underdark

Drow names are defined by the apostrophe (glottal stop) and heavy sibilants (S, SH, Z, X). Female names are longer and more elaborate, reflecting matriarchal society. Male names are short and martial. Every drow carries their noble house name: Vael'ar, Baen'ric.

Zedra Vael'ar — a notable drow name pattern

Vyracon — traditional drow martial name

How to Choose the Right Fantasy Name

Match Race to Sound

A wood elf ranger needs a different sound than a tiefling warlock. Use nature-inspired syllables for elves, harsh guttural stops for orcs, and exotic phonemes for tieflings. The phonetic palette should signal the character's cultural background.

Sound palette guide &rarr;

Consider Class and Background

A gentle healer shouldn't have a brutal-sounding orc name. Let the phonetics reflect the character's personality, role, and cultural background. A noble background calls for longer, more formal names; a folk hero might have a simple, memorable one.

D&D naming guide &rarr;

Say It Out Loud

A good fantasy name should roll off the tongue. If you stumble pronouncing it, your players or readers will too. Test it in dialogue before committing. Our pronunciation guide covers apostrophes, unusual clusters, and accent placement.

Pronunciation guide &rarr;

Test the Full Name

Say the first and last name together. "Aelindor Moonwhisper" works; "Bob Smith" doesn't fit a high elf archmage. Avoid phonetic clashes with other characters in the same party — no two "Thal-" names in one group.

How do I pronounce fantasy names with apostrophes?

Apostrophes in fantasy names typically indicate a glottal stop — a brief pause between syllables. For example, Vael'ar is pronounced "VAY-el-AR" with a slight break after "Vay-el." Our pronunciation guide covers all the rules for unusual letter combinations and accent placement.

What is the difference between wood elf, high elf, and drow names?

Wood elf names use earthy, nature-inspired elements like Oak, Fern, and Thorn. High elf names favor celestial and noble sounds with references to stars, light, and magic. Drow names feature harsh consonants like X, Z, and V with apostrophes, reflecting their Underdark culture.
