About Tiefling Names

The Player's Handbook (p.21) describes tiefling names as falling into three broad categories. This gives tiefling players more naming flexibility than most races — you can choose a name that reflects your infernal heritage, a name that expresses a personal value, or simply a common name.

Three Naming Approaches

Infernal-style names

These names draw from the Infernal language and carry a distinctly otherworldly sound. They suit tieflings who embrace or acknowledge their fiendish bloodline. The PHB provides sample infernal-style names — see the table below.

Virtue names

A virtue name is a single word — typically an English word — that represents a concept the tiefling values. The PHB (p.21) lists examples including Hope, Mercy, Valor, Chastity, Despair, Excellence, Glory, Ideal, Justice, Love, Penance, Reverence, Silence, Tempest, Vengeance, and Zeal. A virtue name is a statement of identity: the tiefling is choosing to be defined by a concept rather than a family line.

Personal names

Many tieflings simply use ordinary personal names, especially those raised among humans with little connection to their infernal heritage. A tiefling named "Marcus" or "Elena" is making a choice — to be defined by their life, not their bloodline.

These three approaches are creative directions, not mutually exclusive categories. A tiefling might use a personal name in daily life and a virtue name as a self-chosen title, or switch between approaches depending on context.

Choosing a Name for Your Tiefling

  • If your tiefling embraces their infernal heritage: An infernal-style name from the PHB table signals that connection. Names like Mordai, Thrax, or Anakis carry a distinctly non-human sound.
  • If your tiefling defines themselves by a value: A virtue name (Hope, Mercy, Valor, Vengeance) makes a statement. This choice is about personality and personal philosophy, not class or profession — a fighter named "Mercy" and a rogue named "Justice" are both valid.
  • If your tiefling lives among humans: A personal name is the simplest choice. A tiefling raised in a human city may have a human name and think little of their bloodline.
  • If your tiefling is between worlds: Some tieflings use a personal name in daily life and a virtue name or infernal name in specific contexts — when introducing themselves to other tieflings, for example.

Infernal Bloodlines (Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes)

Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes (p.21) expands on tiefling lore with specific infernal bloodlines tied to archdevils: Baalzebul, Dispater, Fierna, Glasya, Mammon, Mephistopheles, and Zariel. Each bloodline grants different traits. This is optional content — the PHB tiefling does not require a specific bloodline. If your DM uses these variants, the bloodline may add flavor to your character's naming choice, but it does not impose a naming rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the three types of tiefling names in D&D 5e?

The Player's Handbook (p.21) describes three categories: infernal-style names (drawn from the Infernal language), virtue names (single words like Hope, Mercy, or Valor), and personal names (ordinary names). A tiefling can use any of these approaches.

What is a tiefling virtue name?

A virtue name is a single word representing a concept the tiefling values — examples from the PHB include Hope, Mercy, Valor, Justice, Vengeance, and Zeal. It is a statement of personal identity, not tied to a character class or profession.

Do tieflings have to use infernal names?

No. The PHB explicitly lists personal names as one of the three naming approaches. A tiefling can use a completely ordinary human name if it fits their backstory.

Can I use these names in my D&D campaign?
